01 ✦ About

From the STAND
to the whole shop.

I'm Frenk — 29, born and raised in North Hollywood, the son of Guatemalan and Salvadoran immigrants and a first-gen Latino kid from the Valley. I've been riding since I was 14, and at 15 I became the grom at a small neighborhood bike shop. From there I worked my way up to running an entire shop. At 18 I decided wrenching wasn't a summer job, it was my career — and I've never looked back.

In 2023 I joined Ridepanda as its first mechanic and the fourth person on the Operations team. Six promotions later, I run day-to-day operations for five hubs across San Francisco, Palo Alto, Seattle, and the DMV. I lead Hub Managers and Service Leads, own hiring and development for every hub teammate, and launch new hubs from location search to go-live.

Along the way I grew a delivery operation from me driving bikes around in my Prius to a six-van fleet across three cities. And when the tools we needed didn't exist, I built them — work orders, warranty tracking, career ladders, compensation planning. Those apps run real parts of the business today.

Off the clock I'm a pretty disciplined guy with a lot of hobbies: chess, cycling, hot yoga, and stretching a borderline unreasonable amount. I truly believe in saunas and sensory deprivation tanks. I'll watch any horror movie you put in front of me, and most of my soundtrack is improvisational jazz or reggaeton.

06 ✦ Blog

Notes from
the WORKSHOP

I was 18 when I decided I was going to take being a bike mechanic seriously. I went to college for exactly one day before I knew it wasn't my path. I was already in love with the work: the craftsmanship, the problem-solving, the feeling of handing someone back a bike that rides better than the day they bought it. My parents pleaded with me not to quit school. I followed my gut anyway.

I was incredibly fortunate to learn from amazing mechanics who were willing to share everything they knew, as long as I kept asking questions. So I never stopped asking.

After four years at a local shop near my parents' house in Palmdale, I moved back to LA at 20 with a couple of roommates and kept looking for work. I landed a job at Bike Effect, a prestigious shop in Santa Monica, and my skills leveled up faster than ever. My head hurt every single day from how much I was taking in. I didn't go to college, but this was my classroom, and class was in session five, sometimes six days a week, eight to ten hours a day.

From there I became Lead Tech at South Bay Cycles in Manhattan Beach. After a little over two years, I took the biggest leap yet: I moved to Spain, living in Barcelona and Madrid, to work as a bike mechanic abroad. Four months of interviews later, I'd run out of money and luck, and I came home. Not every leap lands, and I'm still proud I jumped.

Back in the States, I visited my cousin in San Francisco for a weekend and fell for the city and its energy. I decided to stay. I worked at a local bike shop, then took another leap and joined a startup called Ridepanda as its first San Francisco mechanic and the fourth person on the Operations team.

Almost four years later, I've grown from mechanic to Senior Operations Manager. I've hired 30+ operations teammates, moved our San Francisco hub into bigger spaces again and again as we grew, and opened a brand-new hub in Palo Alto.

These days I only wrench on my own bikes, but I'm so glad I bet on becoming a bike tech, because look where it brought me. I'll keep taking leaps on myself and the decisions I make every day.

Because it's fun! Honestly, nothing excites me more than riding my bike, whether that's a 100-mile day, a quick spin to grab coffee, a mountain bike ride, or just rolling across town to meet friends for the night.

It still gives me that same rush every time I clip in, and as long as it does, I'll keep riding.

The best part is always the people. There's nothing better than riding with friends and just enjoying time together on the road.

For the past 14 years, riding has kept me in shape, and it's done wonders for me mentally too. It calms my anxiety, clears my head, and it's been one of the best ways I've found to meet new friends.

And it's not only about my own rides. I love getting more people onto bikes, whether they just want to hang out or they're picking up something new. When it's someone's new bike day, I get to live it all over again through them.

I'm a Type A person through and through. I love being organized, dreaming up new processes, and finding ways to make things run more smoothly.

That's where building tools comes in. As AI has become part of how we work, I've leaned into it to create tools like Path for Advancement, the Ops Compensation Tracker, Warranty Desk, and plenty more, each one making life easier for my team and our leadership.

I genuinely love tinkering with ways to make things better, especially from an operations perspective. Every spreadsheet that slows a team down is an opportunity.

Looking to build a new tool? I've got you. I'm confident we can build something great together. Let's talk →
